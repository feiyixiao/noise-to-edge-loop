Why this project

Production labels are never perfect, and the model still has to fit on a small device.

Three questions, one pipeline

  • How much Dice does a small segmentation model lose when part of its training masks are wrong in known ways?
  • How much of that loss does an automatic audit win back by removing suspicious cases?
  • What does post-training INT8 cost in Dice, and what does it buy in size and CPU latency?

How I kept it honest

  • Predictions P1 to P7 were written in a design file before the main run.
  • Split by patient case, never by slice. Test masks are always clean.
  • One pilot, one main run, no tuning on the test cases.
  • Every number here comes from a results CSV. Failures stay in.

What was built

Small on purpose: 2D slices, CPU only, public data.

Data

MSD Task04 Hippocampus, 260 cases, binary mask. 2D axial slices padded to 64×64. Split by case: 160 train, 40 validation, 60 test.

Label damage

Wrong size, shifted mask or extra island, chosen at random per case, each set to Dice 0.8 against the clean mask. 20% or 40% of training cases are damaged. Validation and test masks stay clean.

Models

A: small U-Net from scratch, 4 levels, base 16 channels.
B: U-Net with an ImageNet ResNet18 encoder. A pretrained encoder, not a foundation model.

Audit filter

An existing detector scores every training case from its image and mask. The top share equal to the nominal noise rate is dropped, without looking at which cases were really damaged.

Edge step

ONNX export, ONNX Runtime static INT8 with 100 training slices, timed on one CPU thread at batch size 1.

Why the gap is so large

500 randomised ellipsoid volumes (random pose, size, contrast, blur and noise) are much easier to see than a real hippocampus.

Contrast-to-noise inside versus outside the mask: 2.41 synthetic against 0.55 real.

Synthetic pretraining plus fine-tuning beat 20 real cases alone by +0.0105 on average, but not in every seed (+0.0145, +0.0173, −0.0003). I report it as a weak positive.
